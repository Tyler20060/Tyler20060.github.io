Types of Colour Blindness

Home Types Causes & Genetics Living with It Design Accessibility Resources & Support

Colour blindness comes in several forms, each affecting vision differently. Most people with colour blindness have red-green colour blindness, while blue-yellow and complete colour blindness are much rarer.

Overview: The Main Types

🔴🟢 Red-Green Colour Blindness

Most Common Type

Difficulty distinguishing red from green

Affects: ~6% of males, 0.4% of females

🔵🟡 Blue-Yellow Colour Blindness

Rare Type

Difficulty distinguishing blue from yellow

Affects: ~0.001% equally in males and females

⚪ Complete Colour Blindness

Extremely Rare Type

Cannot perceive any color (grayscale vision)

Affects: ~0.003% equally in males and females

Red-Green Colour Blindness

The Most Common Form

Red-green colour blindness accounts for approximately 99% of all colour blindness cases. It primarily affects males due to its X-linked inheritance pattern.

Protanopia (Red-Blind)

🔴 Protanopia Characteristics:

What It Means:

Missing or non-functional long-wavelength (red) cones

How It Affects Vision:
  • Red colors appear as dark brown or black
  • Orange and yellow appear more yellow
  • Green appears more bluish
  • Overall vision may be slightly dimmer
  • Can see approximately 500 hues (vs 10,000,000 in normal vision)
Frequency:
  • ~1% of males
  • ~0.02% of females

Deuteranopia (Green-Blind)

🟢 Deuteranopia Characteristics:

What It Means:

Missing or non-functional medium-wavelength (green) cones

How It Affects Vision:
  • Green colors appear more reddish or brownish
  • Red appears more orange or brown
  • Yellow appears more orange
Characteristic Protanopia (Red-Blind) Deuteranopia (Green-Blind)
Missing Cone Type L-cones (long wavelength/red) M-cones (medium wavelength/green)
Overall Brightness Slightly darker vision Normal brightness

Blue-Yellow Colour Blindness

🔵 Tritanopia Characteristics:

What It Means:

Missing or non-functional short-wavelength (blue) cones

How It Affects Vision:
  • Blue appears more like pink or purple
  • Yellow appears pinkish or light gray
  • Can see red and green relatively well

Complete Colour Blindness (Achromatopsia/Monochromacy)

⚪ Achromatopsia Characteristics:

What It Means:

Absence of all three types of color cones OR all cones are non-functional

How It Affects Vision:
  • Complete absence of color perception
  • Vision is entirely grayscale (black, white, grays)
  • All colors appear as shades of gray
