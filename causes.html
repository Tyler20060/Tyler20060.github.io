Most colour blindness is inherited through genes. Understanding the genetic basis helps explain why it's more common in men and how it runs in families.

The Biology of Color Vision

How Eyes Detect Color

The human eye contains millions of light-sensitive cells called photoreceptors. There are two main types:

📍 Rods and Cones:

Rods (Rod Cells):
  • About 120 million per eye
  • Detect light and dark (grayscale)
  • Very sensitive to low light
  • Responsible for night vision
Cones (Cone Cells):
  • About 6-7 million per eye
  • Detect color
  • Concentrated in the macula (center of vision)
  • Three types, each sensitive to different wavelengths

The Three Types of Cones

People with normal color vision have three types of cone cells:

S-Cones (Short)

Color: Blue

Wavelength: 420 nm (short waves)

Function: Detect blue colors

M-Cones (Medium)

Color: Green

Wavelength: 530 nm (medium waves)

Function: Detect green colors

L-Cones (Long)

Color: Red

Wavelength: 560 nm (long waves)

Function: Detect red colors

Genetic Inheritance

X-Linked Inheritance (Red-Green)

Red-green colour blindness follows an X-linked recessive inheritance pattern. This is why it's more common in men:

🧬 Why Men Are More Affected:

Chromosome Basics:
  • Men: XY chromosomes (one X, one Y)
  • Women: XX chromosomes (two X chromosomes)
  • The colour blindness gene is on the X chromosome
The Math:
  • Man with gene: X^c Y = Colour blind (only one X, so gene is expressed)
  • Woman with gene: X^c X = Carrier (other X chromosome is normal)
  • Woman to be colour blind: X^c X^c = Needs gene on BOTH X chromosomes (very rare)

Probability of Inheritance

If mother is a carrier (X^C X^c):
  • 50% chance sons will be colour blind
  • 50% chance daughters will be carriers
If father is colour blind (X^c Y):
  • All daughters will be carriers (inherit father's X^c)
  • All sons will have normal vision (inherit X^C from mother)

Genetic Testing

Genetic Testing for Colour Blindness:

DNA Analysis:
  • Blood sample or saliva collection
  • DNA extracted from cells
  • Specific genes sequenced and analyzed
  • Mutations identified and documented

Key Takeaways

  • ✅ Color blindness is genetic in most cases, caused by missing or faulty cone genes
  • ✅ Red-green is X-linked - why men are more commonly affected
  • ✅ Two genes (OPN1LW and OPN1MW) cause red-green colour blindness
  • ✅ Genetic testing can confirm diagnosis and identify specific mutations
